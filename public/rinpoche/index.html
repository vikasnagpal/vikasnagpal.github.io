THE JOURNEY WITHIN

Upper Mustang remains one of the last places where traditional Tibetan life continues undisturbed by modern hurry. For thirteen days, we ride through wind-sculpted valleys and mountain passes up to 3,840 metres alongside HE Palga Rinpoche. The route is demanding, but the true journey is quieter: leaving the noise behind and letting the space of the high desert clear the mind.

A sacred crossing through the high desert of the Kali Gandaki.
Where every mile on the road becomes a quiet journey within.

The Monk Who Sold
His Maserati

Travel the high trails of Upper Mustang in small, steady company with HE Palga Rinpoche. Mornings begin with simple breathwork and stretching. Days are spent crossing cold mountain passes and stony riverbeds. By evening, we gather in quiet village lodges for unhurried conversations on focus, detachment, and living with fewer burdens. Supported by seasoned scouts and mechanics, the ride itself becomes an exercise in staying present.

The Way Forward

Over thirteen days, we follow the Kali Gandaki from the subtropical waters of Pokhara into the rain shadow of the Himalayas, climbing towards the high desert of Upper Mustang.

As the valley climbs, the pace of the day slows down. Fast highways give way to riverbed crossings, red-walled canyons, and walled villages that have guarded these trade routes for generations. Nothing is hurried. We reach Lo Manthang at 3,840 metres, take time to sit with the landscape, and return south with a clearer perspective.

DAY 03 · ALT 2,670 m · NIGHT MARPHA

Pokhara to Marpha

The old salt caravan route.

A long day north into the Kali Gandaki gorge. Pavement ends past Beni; the track turns to riverbed gravel and rocky switchbacks. Arrive by afternoon at Marpha, a whitewashed Thakali village of tight stone alleys, apple orchards, and dry mountain winds.

SPACE TO PAUSE

DAY 04 · ALT 3,760 m · NIGHT MUKTINATH

Muktinath

Fire and water in the same place.

A short but exposed ride through the wind-scoured Jomsom valley, past the medieval gate of Kagbeni. We climb to Muktinath at 3,760 metres, a revered pilgrimage site for both Hindus and Buddhists, where a natural flame has flickered over an underground spring for centuries.

SPACE TO PAUSE

Dusk at the temple. Walking quietly around the outer shrines as the wind picks up off the snowfields.

FIELD NOTE

“108 brass water spouts in the biting wind. The natural flame flickers undisturbed over cold spring water.”

Into Upper Mustang

The restricted kingdom begins.

Ride down through Kagbeni, where the restricted permit gate opens. The valley shifts into ochre cliffs, wind-carved canyons, and green barley patches clustered around white chortens. Over the Chele pass and rocky switchbacks into Syangboche.

Late afternoon on the pass. Complete silence as the sun drops behind Dhaulagiri.

Lo Manthang

The ancient walled capital of Lo.

A full day inside the walled capital. We walk the narrow earthen alleys between whitewashed homes and step inside Jampa Lhakhang, beneath towering golden statues and centuries-old murals. In the afternoon, Guruji leads an unhurried conversation in the quiet monastery courtyard.

DAY 08 · ALT 3,840 m · NIGHT LO MANTHANG

Chhoser Sky Caves

Five storeys carved into sheer cliff.

A short ride north toward the border of Tibet. We explore the Jhong Cave complex at Chhoser, forty chambers carved five storeys high into vertical cliffs thousands of years ago. Later, we visit Nyphu Cave Gompa, built seamlessly into the canyon stone.

SPACE TO PAUSE

Cool shadow inside the cave rooms, looking out through arrow slits into the bright white sunlight.

FIELD NOTE

“Climbing notched tree trunks into upper chambers. Wind whistles through the openings like a flute.”

DAY 09 · ALT 2,980 m · NIGHT CHHUSANG

Luri Cave Gompa

Earthen spires and hidden sanctuaries.

We ride east into the fluted sandstone pinnacles of the Puyung valley to reach Luri Gompa. This solitary monastery is carved directly into a natural spire of rock, housing 14th-century Mahasiddha frescoes untouched by modern restoration.

SPACE TO PAUSE

The steep foot climb up the sandstone needle. A solitary monk turning a heavy brass key in the wooden lock.

FIELD NOTE

“A dome carved inside an earthen spire. Intricate 14th-century frescoes lit only by filtered daylight.”

Leaving the desert, entering pine forests.
